WHY IT MATTERS

Waste doesn’t
just disappear.

Uncollected rubbish can attract pests, create unpleasant surroundings, and block drainage when it is washed into waterways. Burning mixed waste can also expose nearby people to harmful smoke.

Keeping waste managed is not only about appearances. It supports healthier homes, safer public spaces, and a neighbourhood people can take pride in.

PROJECT FORGE · KARIOBANGI

A community challenge,
we can solve together.

This project starts with residents’ everyday experience and turns it into shared action, practical services, and measurable progress.

01 / THE COMMUNITY CHALLENGE

Residents of Kariobangi face significant challenges in managing household and community waste. Inadequate waste collection services, limited disposal facilities, illegal dumping, and low awareness of proper waste management practices contribute to the accumulation of waste in public spaces. This affects environmental cleanliness, public health, and the overall quality of life within the community.

02 / PROBLEM STATEMENT

Residents of Kariobangi struggle with proper waste management and disposal, resulting in environmental pollution, blocked drainage systems, increased health risks, unpleasant living conditions, and environmental degradation.

03 / INITIAL HYPOTHESIS

We believe that poor waste disposal in Kariobangi is caused by inadequate waste collection services, insufficient waste disposal infrastructure, low awareness of proper waste management practices, and limited community participation. If residents are provided with accessible waste disposal solutions, waste management education, and opportunities to participate in recycling and clean-up initiatives, then waste disposal practices will improve and the community will become cleaner, healthier, and more sustainable.

04 / POTENTIAL BENEFICIARIES

Primary: households, children, women, waste collectors, and informal recyclers in Kariobangi.

Secondary: schools, local businesses, health facilities, community organizations, and local government agencies that benefit from a cleaner and healthier environment.

A WAY FORWARD

Small actions.
Collective change.

Cleaner streets take more than one person. These are practical starting points for households, local groups, businesses, and service providers.

01

Separate at the source

Keep food scraps, recyclables, and other waste apart where possible. Clean, separated materials are easier to recover and reuse.

↗
02

Use agreed collection points

Find out when and where waste is collected locally, and keep bags contained until collection instead of leaving them in drains or open spaces.

↗
03

Organise neighbourhood clean-ups

Residents, schools, and traders can work together on regular clean-ups and identify recurring dumping hotspots.

↗
04

Make collection reliable

Community leaders and service providers can share clear schedules, report missed pick-ups, and plan accessible disposal points.
